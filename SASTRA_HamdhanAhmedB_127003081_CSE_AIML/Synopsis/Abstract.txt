Modern automotive systems rely heavily on the AUTOSAR (AUTomotive Open System ARchitecture) standard to specify complex electronic control unit (ECU) software architectures. However, reviewing and verifying multi-hundred-page High-Level Design (HLD) specifications remains a manual, error-prone, and labor-intensive challenge. Furthermore, transmitting proprietary automotive intellectual property to public cloud AI models is strictly prohibited by automotive cybersecurity regulations (ISO/SAE 21434). This project introduces AUTOSAR AI, an on-premises document intelligence platform that automates the ingestion, structural entity extraction, architectural rule validation, and grounded conversational analysis of automotive HLDs. The system combines PyMuPDF for page-preserving extraction, SentenceTransformers (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) and ChromaDB for dense vector retrieval, in-memory BM25 for exact automotive identifier matching, and a local quantized Large Language Model (Ollama llama3.2:3b) for grounded question-answering with verifiable citations. Additionally, an independent Grounding Service decomposes generated responses into verifiable propositions to calculate a quantitative Grounding Score, and security defenses neutralize CSV formula injection attacks. The platform was validated through 28 automated pytest test cases (100% pass rate) and a 35-query benchmark evaluation.
